J1 First results

Most published numbers count the chip and stop.

Energy figures for local models rarely agree, because each one measures something different: package power, a TDP estimate, one device in one paper. We measure at two points on the board and say which is which.

TP2 is the chip. TP1 is the board's DC input, which includes the memory. The gap between them is the part most benchmarks leave out.

J2 Research

Fewer bits is not fewer joules.

We quantized one model, Qwen2.5 1.5B, into eight formats from the same F16 weights and measured each one: decode energy at the chip and at the board, and perplexity on WikiText-2.

Measured at the chip, F16 looks as cheap as 4-bit. It is not. The chip sits waiting on memory, and the memory traffic shows up only at the board, where F16 costs 2.1 times what Q4_0 does. Q5_K_M costs more than Q6_K and is worse. Q4_0 matches Q4_K_M within error for 18% less energy.

The second request is the expensive one.

Serving several requests at once should spread the cost of reading the weights. It does, but not evenly. At batch 1 the GPU draws 17 to 24 W, waiting on memory. At batch 2 it jumps to its ceiling of 33 to 36 W and stays there. So the second sequence pays for almost the whole jump, and every sequence after it is close to free.

On Q4_0, batch 2 saves 1% per token. Batch 8 saves 72%.

J3 Method

Small enough to read in one sitting.

joule on GitHub →
  1. Baseline first. Ten seconds of idle power before anything runs, so every figure can be reported gross and net.
  2. Phases apart. Prefill (512 prompt tokens) and decode (128 generated tokens) run as separate tests.
  3. Sized runs. A short probe sets the repetition count so each phase has about twenty timed seconds.
  4. Steady window only. The first quarter of the compute window is dropped for clock ramp and sensor lag.
  5. Watts over throughput. Mean power across the window divided by measured tokens per second gives joules per token.

J4 How we work

A company, with the research in the open.

The methods, the code and the raw measurements are public. Anyone can rerun a number on this page and get the same answer or tell us why not.

The company is the work around them: making a model run well on a specific board, and measuring it honestly when it does. If you build hardware or ship models to devices and want that done, write to us through GitHub.

J5 What these numbers are not

Known limits, stated up front.

Not wall power

TP1 is DC input as the SMC reports it. It leaves out the adapter's own loss. A wall meter is next.

Not fully settled

0.5B decode drifted from 58 to 74 mJ per token across three runs while throughput held. The table shows what we measured, drift included.

One machine so far

Apple Silicon only. MLX and a 7B sweep are running now; boards with NPUs come next, which are the hardware served worst today.
