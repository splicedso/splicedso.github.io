Note 23 · 25 October 2026

Does a clock cap pay per correct answer?

Capping the Strix Halo's shader clock at 1,800 MHz cuts joules per correct answer 19 to 28% on a 7B and a 32B at eight requests at once, with accuracy within a point, for 11 to 24% less speed. Two-bit's lead over 4-bit shrinks under the cap.

Prediction verdict: held on the headline (15 to 28% fewer joules per correct answer at accuracy within a point, every cell); missed on the 32B calibrated Q2_K, which slows 22 to 24% under the cap (predicted under 10%) and whose lead over Q4_0 shrinks to 9.7% on the mean (7.6% and 12.0% per pass) (predicted 16% within 5 points).

Spliced research note 23, 25 October 2026. AMD Ryzen AI Max+ 395 (Strix Halo, Radeon 8060S, 128 GB, shared GPU memory 90%), Linux, llama.cpp 620d2225c, Vulkan. Qwen2.5 7B and 32B Instruct.

The clock-cap sweep (results/halo-clockcap.md) found that capping the shader clock at 1,800 MHz through the OD table cuts the 7B's batch-1 decode energy per token by 37% at 4% less speed. That is energy per token at batch 1. The bar every model and setting is judged on is joules per correct answer at 8 requests at once (experiments/quality.py, note 19). This note runs that harness under the cap, on the same three cells note 22 measured on auto earlier today.

Prediction (docs/predictions.md, committed and pushed before the first measurement): accuracy within 1 point of the auto run in every cell. 7B Q4_0: 20 to 35% fewer joules per correct answer, 5 to 20% fewer tokens per second (eight slots are more compute-bound than batch-1 decode, so the speed cost is larger than the sweep's 4%). 32B calibrated Q2_K: 25 to 40% fewer joules, under 10% slower. 32B Q4_0: 25 to 40% fewer joules, and calibrated Q2_K stays 16% cheaper than Q4_0 within 5 points. The cap holds for all six runs.

Method

experiments/halo_capped_quality.sh: the OD table set to 1,800 MHz (power_dpm_force_performance_level=manual, pp_od_clk_voltage s 1 1800, c), verified under a decode load before anything is measured, the active clock sampled during every run and its maximum logged. Then quality.py, GSM8K-250, 8 slots, chip (package) energy, two passes with the second reversed: 7B Q4_0, 32B calibrated Q2_K, 32B Q4_0. Package idle sampled for 10 s before every run with nothing loaded (results/halo-capped-idle-pre.csv), next to quality.py's own loaded idle. Auto comparison cells are note 22's runs from earlier today, same machine, same build, same shared-memory setting; the 7B auto pair was re-run in this session (below) so both of its passes share conditions. The clock was restored to auto and the OD table reset at the end; readback in results/halo-capped-readback.txt.

Package power during the runs fell from 122 to 127 W on auto to 75 to 87 W under the cap. The cap held: the active clock, sampled every 0.5 s, never exceeded 1,800 MHz in any run. Idle before each run drifted 6.3 to 19.2 W; gross J per correct answer is the headline, net is soft.

Spread: the 7B and 32B Q4_0 cells repeat to within 2.0% and 4.8% on J per correct (the latter flagged: 87.0 against 83.2 W at the same speed). The capped 32B Q2_K pair differs by 9.7%, flagged: the power log shows the first 15 minutes of its pass 2 at 64 to 67 W at the same clock and GPU load as pass 1, then 75 to 87 W, and the pre-run idle before it was 6.3 W where every other run saw 10.6 to 19.2 W. Something outside the GPU was in a lower power state for that stretch; the logger now records memory, fabric and display clocks and the display's power state so the next such stretch can be attributed. Pass 1 (403.4 J per correct) is the representative Q2_K figure; the mean carries the artefact.

The re-run reproduced pass 1: Q4_0 within 1% (139.8 against 139.6 W, 160.5 against 161.6 J/correct); Q2_K within 3% on power and energy per token (132.0 against 135.1 W, 741 against 717 mJ/token), with six fewer problems right, which puts its J/correct 6.7% off. So note 22's 10% spread is run order, not the session: pass 2 followed four 32B runs and drew 10% less power at the same speed, and a first 7B run after a change of state (a session start; here, 95 minutes under the clock cap ending a minute earlier) draws the higher figure. The flag stays on note 22's 7B cells.

What to take from this

  1. The cap pays on the bar that matters. At 8 requests at once, every cell costs 15 to 28% fewer joules per correct answer than on auto, at the same accuracy. The 7B Q4_0 drops from 153.8 to 111.3 J per correct answer at 15% fewer tokens per second; the 32B Q4_0 from 563.6 to 426.2 at 11% fewer. The predicted direction and sizes held for both Q4_0 cells.
  2. The 2-bit model pays for the cap in speed. Calibrated Q2_K at 32B slows 24% under the cap where Q4_0 slows 11%, and its energy lead over Q4_0 shrinks from 16% to 9.7% (7.6% and 12.0% per pass). Fewer bits mean more unpacking work per weight, and a lower shader clock makes that work the slow part. Not predicted; the prediction said the 16% lead would hold within 5 points.
  3. Serving at 8 slots costs more speed than batch-1 decode did. The clock-cap sweep lost 4% of batch-1 decode speed at 1,800 MHz; here the 7B loses 15% and the 32B Q4_0 11%, as predicted, because eight concurrent requests make the run more compute-bound.
  4. For Foundry on this machine: a capped 32B Q4_0 answers GSM8K at 95.8% for 426 J per correct answer, 24% less than the same model on auto, at 11% fewer tokens per second; whether that trade is worth it is a product decision, and only the energy and speed are measured here.

Limits

One task, one machine, chip (package) energy, one cap value. The cap needs root; a laptop's equivalent is its firmware power mode, not measured here. Whether a lower cap (1,200 MHz) does better per correct answer at 8 slots is untested.
