Does 2-bit pay on large models?

Calibrated 2-bit against Q4_0 at 7B and 32B on the Strix Halo. At 32B the 2-bit model costs 16% less per correct answer at equal accuracy; at 7B Q4_0 still wins by 21%. The crossover is where memory traffic takes over the token.

Prediction verdict: held in direction at both sizes; the 32B margin (16.0%) is outside the "less than 15%" pre-registered.

Spliced research note 22, 25 October 2026. AMD Ryzen AI Max+ 395 (Strix Halo, Radeon 8060S, 128 GB, shared GPU memory 90%), Linux, llama.cpp 620d2225c, Vulkan. Qwen2.5 7B and 32B Instruct.

Note 20 found that on the M5 Pro a calibrated 2-bit 1.5B doubles the accuracy of a plain one but still costs more per correct answer than Q4_0, because a 2-bit token costs more energy there. The reason given was that fewer bits only save energy where moving bytes dominates. Larger models are where they should: the weights read per token grow with the model.

Prediction (docs/predictions.md, committed before the runs): on the 7B, Q4_0 is still cheaper per correct answer. On the 32B, calibrated Q2_K loses fewer accuracy points than the 1.5B did and costs less energy per token than Q4_0; it wins per correct answer, by less than 15%.

Method

For each model, an importance matrix from 1,500 GSM8K training problems with their worked answers (note 20's recipe), used to quantize the F16 model to Q2_K; standard Q4_0 for comparison. experiments/quality.py, the first 250 GSM8K test questions, 8 requests at once, two passes with the second reversed, chip (APU package) energy with Phase's warm-up, nothing else on the GPU.

Results

Two passes each, the second in reverse order, means below; every pass and both idle readings are in results/lattice-halo.md, raw JSON in results/lattice-halo-*-pass{1,2}.json.

32B:

format correct J per correct net of idle tok/s mJ per token
Q4_0 94.8% (237, 237) 563.6 467.7 56.3 2224
Q2_K, calibrated 95.6% (240, 238) 473.7 393.6 61.2 1992

7B:

format correct J per correct net of idle tok/s mJ per token
Q4_0 88.6% (220, 223) 153.8 131.2 225.9 589
Q2_K, calibrated 84.2% (212, 209) 195.9 160.7 187.3 695

Spread: the 32B cells repeat to within 2.8% on J per correct (Q4_0's mJ per token differs by 3.3% between passes, flagged). The 7B cells do not: pass 1 drew 10% more power than pass 2 at the same speed, so Q4_0's J per correct differs by 10.2% between passes and Q2_K's by 4.5%, both flagged. Pass 1 was re-run in this session (16:00, results/lattice-halo.md) and reproduced pass 1 within 1% on Q4_0 (139.8 against 139.6 W, 160.5 against 161.6 J per correct) and within 3% on Q2_K's power and energy per token, so the gap is run order, not the session: pass 2 followed four 32B runs and drew 10% less power at the same speed. The flag stays; the 7B ranking is far outside the gap. Idle drifted 16.6 to 25.9 W over the afternoon; the chip totals do not depend on it. Each 32B run took 15 to 18 minutes.

What to take from this

  1. On the 32B, calibrated 2-bit pays. 473.7 against 563.6 J per correct answer, 16.0% less, at equal accuracy (95.6% against 94.8%, two problems apart) and 9% more tokens per second. The 2-bit token costs 10% less energy than the 4-bit one here (1992 against 2224 mJ). The prediction's direction held; its margin did not: 16.0% on the two-pass mean (17.9% and 14.0% per pass) is outside the "less than 15%" written down beforehand.
  2. On the 7B, Q4_0 still wins on every axis: 4.4 points more accurate, 21% less energy per correct answer (calibrated Q2_K costs 27% more), 21% faster. The 7B half of the prediction held, as it did on the 1.5B (note 20).
  3. The crossover is between 7B and 32B on this machine. At 7B a 2-bit token costs 18% more chip energy than a 4-bit one; at 32B, which reads four times the bytes per token, it costs 10% less. Fewer bits pay once memory traffic dominates the token, and not before.
  4. Calibration holds accuracy at both sizes: 84.2% against 88.6% at 7B, and no loss at 32B. The 1.5B lost 24 points (note 20). Larger models quantize more gracefully, as predicted.

Limits

One task, one machine, chip (package) energy. Calibration on GSM8K's training split matches the test's domain; note 20 measured how much that adds on the 1.5B.
